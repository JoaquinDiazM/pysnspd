## Constitutive source and second law

At fixed node, $f_k=f_{\rm FD}(E_k,T_E)$, $\epsilon_k=4u^*w_ke_k$ and $\sigma_k=(\epsilon_k k_B/E_k)\log[(1-p_k)/p_k]$.

$$
a_k=f_k(1-p_k),\quad b_k=p_k(1-f_k),\qquad
h_k^+=\frac{E_ka_k}{\sum_l\epsilon_lE_la_l},\quad
h_k^-=\frac{E_kb_k}{\sum_l\epsilon_lE_lb_l},\quad
H_k=P h_k^{\operatorname{sgn}P}.
$$

Thus $\sum_k\epsilon_kH_k=P$. At $p_k=0$, cooling vanishes; at $p_k=1$, heating vanishes. The RHS needs no logarithm or clipping at endpoints. At FD, both carriers equal $(\partial_Tf_k)/C_{\rm FD}$.

Energy-equivalent temperature implies $\sum_k\epsilon_k(a_k-b_k)=0$. Since $\log(a_k/b_k)=\log[(1-p_k)/p_k]-E_k/(k_BT_E)$, the inequalities $a\log(a/b)\ge a-b$ and $b\log(a/b)\le a-b$ give

$$
\dot S_{\rm exc}=P\left(\sum_k\sigma_kh_k^{\operatorname{sgn}P}-T_E^{-1}\right)\ge0,\qquad
\dot S_{\rm source}-\dot G_T=(Q_\Delta+P_J)/T_E+\dot S_{\rm exc}.
$$

The endpoint entropy statement uses one-sided limits. This is an effective heat-distribution hypothesis, not a recovered microscopic collision integral. BGK conserves the same energy moment and does not duplicate the heat source.
